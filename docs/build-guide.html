How to use this guide

  1. Do Phase 0 first. It creates the project, installs everything and puts the project brief where Claude Code reads it.
  2. Start a fresh Claude Code session in the project folder for each phase. Paste that phase's prompt.
  3. Check the Done when list on your phone before moving on. Then mark the phase done here.
  4. Keep docs/prototype.html in the project. It is the visual and behavioral reference, and its plan() function is a working version of the planner.

Expo changes every release. The project's AGENTS.md tells Claude Code to read the versioned docs for SDK 58 before touching any Expo API. Every prompt below repeats that, because it prevents the most common mistakes.

Fastest route to something you use daily: Phases 0, 1, 2, 3, 4 and 6. That gives you the planner, the screens, the living sky, the pinned notification with proof, and rewards. Then Phases 17 and 18 for the proof wall website. Do Phases 5, 7, 8 and the add-ons after a week of real use. Skip Phase 11 (focus lock) until everything else feels solid.

Planner: how the day gets built

    
The planner is one pure function. It reruns every time an anchor is tapped, a task is added or completed, or the clock moves. These cases become your first tests in Phase 1.

    

      
07:30 / 10:15Default wake / leave for office, used until you tap

      
19:30 / 23:30Default home / sleep

      
20 minGet-ready buffer after wake and after home. 20 before leaving. 30 before sleep.

      
10 minGap between tasks. Windows under 10 minutes are dropped.

    

    

      
#Situation (weekday)Expected result

      
        
1Woke 07:42, clock 07:50Morning window 08:02 to 09:55. Workout (30 min) fits there.

        
2Tap "I'm home" at 17:40Evening opens at 18:00 instead of the predicted 19:50. Tonight gains 110 minutes. Tasks that went to tomorrow pull back in.

        
3Woke at 09:30Morning window is 5 minutes, so it is dropped. The workout moves to the evening.

        
4Tap "Leaving for office"Every unfinished morning task moves to tonight. Evening times show as predicted (~) until home is tapped.

        
5Tap "Going to sleep"All remaining tasks go to tomorrow. Nothing is placed.

        
6Saturday, no officeThree windows (wake+20 to 13:00, 14:00 to 18:30, 19:30 to sleep−30). Routines such as laundry and cleaning appear first.

        
7More work than evening timeLowest priority tasks go to "later". Must-do tasks are placed first.

        
8Calendar event 20:00 to 21:00 (Phase 5)That hour is removed from the evening window. Tasks flow around it.

Design: the sky is the interface

    
The whole app tints to the time of your day: soft blush after waking, misty blue at the office, rose at dusk, deep calm blue at night. Every transition is slow and has no bounce. The goal is that opening the app feels like a breath, not an alarm.

    

    

      
Easingcubic-bezier(.22, .8, .3, 1) everywhere. No overshoot, no shake.

      
DurationsSky cross-fade 2.2s · sun and moon 2.6s · rows re-plan 0.9s · sheets 0.8s

      
AmbientTimer ring breathes (6s). Next-up ring pulses (3.4s). Clouds drift (40s).

      
RewardCheck draws itself, XP counts up, soft glow motes float up and fade. No confetti burst.

      
TypeBricolage Grotesque for headings, Figtree for text, DM Mono for times.

      
AccessibilityRespect reduce-motion. Keep text contrast at 4.5:1 or better on cards.

Two of these have honest limits. Stakes: a friend message can only be sent while the app is able to run, so the check happens when you tap sleep, when you next open the app, and in a background task at 22:30. Focus lock: Expo has no built-in app blocker. It needs a small custom Android module and two special permissions, so it works in your own APK and not on the Play Store. Both are marked in their phases.

Things that will bite you

    
    
      
  • The pinned notification is best-effort. Android 14 and later can let users dismiss "ongoing" notifications from ordinary apps, and some phone makers kill background apps. Phase 4 adds a repeating reminder and re-posts on app open. Test on your own phone before trusting it.
    • 
      
  • Battery optimization. Set the app to "Unrestricted" in Android battery settings, or reminders arrive late.
    • 
      
  • Notification permission. Android 13+ asks at runtime, and only after a notification channel exists. Create the channel first.
    • 
      
  • Calendar and camera permissions are runtime prompts. Handle "denied" with a plain message, not a crash.
    • 
      
  • Your API key lives on the phone. Fine for you alone. Anyone with the phone and some effort can read it. Never put it in the repo.
    • 
      
  • Dates. Store times as minutes since midnight plus a local date string. Do not use UTC for "today".
    • 
      
  • Proof photos are private data. They stay in the app's own folder. Export is manual.
    • 
      
  • Focus lock can lock you out of your own phone. It must always have a hold-to-exit and must never block calls or emergency contacts. Test it with the phone in your hand, not at 2 a.m.
    • 
      
  • Stake messages go to another person. Show the exact text first, allow turning it off, and only ever send for tasks you explicitly staked.
    • 
      
  • Voice notes are private data and need the microphone permission. Keep them on the device.
    • 
      
  • Public means public. Anyone can see, copy and screenshot what you post, and search engines will index it. Photos of your room show your home. The posting delay stops the page showing where you are right now. Do not photograph other people without asking, and never post documents, message screens or anything with an address.
    •
